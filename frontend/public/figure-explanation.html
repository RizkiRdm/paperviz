The problem

Charts assume you already know the field

Research figures are designed for domain experts. Unlabeled axes, discipline-specific conventions, and missing context make them opaque to students and cross-discipline readers.

  • Complex axes and legends require domain knowledge to decode
  • Statistical notation hides the actual finding
  • Figures reference methods explained pages earlier
  • Students skip figures — then miss the key evidence

The solution

Plain-language explanations with context

PaperViz analyzes the figure within the context of the full paper. You get an explanation that tells you what the chart shows, what the numbers mean, and why the finding matters.

  • Explains axes, legends, and data points in plain language
  • Provides context from the surrounding paper text
  • Identifies the key takeaway without jargon
  • Links back to the source paper for verification

Example

What you get

Input

A bar chart showing "Cognitive Load Index by Sleep Group" with three bars, error bars, and a p-value annotation

PaperViz explanation

What this chart shows

This bar chart compares how mentally demanding tasks were for three groups: people who slept well, people who slept poorly, and people who were sleep-deprived. The y-axis shows the Cognitive Load Index (higher = more mentally taxing). The poor-sleep group scored about 40% higher than the good-sleep group, and this difference was statistically significant (p < 0.01), meaning it's unlikely to be due to chance.

Honest limitations

What PaperViz can and can't do

Current limitations

  • Works with text-layer PDFs — scanned/image-only PDFs not supported yet
  • Explanations are interpretations, not definitive readings — check the original
  • Highly specialized figures (e.g., crystallography, proteomics) may need expert review
  • Re-visualized charts are simplified representations, not exact replicas
